“The Lighthouse Keeper’s Ledger”
There was once a keeper who trusted ink more than memory.
Her tower stood on no coast. Ships found it by name, not by light, and the name was the same for thousands of other keepers, each with a lamp of their own.
She never threw anything away. Each night she added a line to the record, and each line sat on top of the last, so the whole history of the work could be read back like rings in a tree.
Visitors came and went. Some only watched. Some left a small mark to say they had passed. A few borrowed her plans, bent them to their own shores, and returned them with a quiet request. The one who treasurers, treasures the whole 
She never asked who they were. She only asked whether the work was better for it.
When she was asked where the tower was, she would say, “Wherever someone is willing to open the door.”
