years walking constantly and writing prolifically, even as he was periodically called up for Swiss military service during the First World War, and his famous story The Walk appeared in 1924, followed by a move to Bern in 1927, where he briefly worked as an assistant in the state archive before drifting through cheap rooming houses, and around this time he developed his “pencil method,” drafting texts in pencil in a tiny script, sometimes only a millimeter or two high, known as the “microscripts,” written on scraps such as calendar pages, envelopes, and the backs of cards, a method he later said helped free him from a block he felt with the pen, until in 1936, after a breakdown, his sister Lisa had him admitted to a sanatorium in Waldau, near Bern, and in 1940 he was transferred to a sanatorium in Herisau, where he essentially stopped writing and reportedly said he was there not to write but to be mad, though the writer Carl Seelig befriended him and took him on long walks recorded in Walks with Robert Walser, and Walser died on New Year’s Eve, 1963, during a snowy walk near Herisau, a photograph of his body in the snow being one of the best-known images of him, and decades later scholars deciphered the microscripts, revealing hundreds of unpublished pages, while writers like Kafka, Hesse, Sebald, and Sontag are counted among his admirers.
